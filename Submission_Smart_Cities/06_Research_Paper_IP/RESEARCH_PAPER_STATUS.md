## What the paper contributes

This is the project's **unique outcome**, reported in Section 6.3 of the project report.

Soil-image datasets are commonly built by expanding a handful of source photographs into
thousands of samples through cropping and augmentation, and published accuracies for the
task routinely exceed 90 %. The paper evaluates the same classifier, on identical features,
under two protocols:

- **Augmentation-level** — the conventional approach, shuffling all expanded samples before
  splitting
- **Source-disjoint** — every sample derived from a held-out photograph excluded from
  training

| Task | Augmentation-level | Source-disjoint | Gap |
|---|---|---|---|
| Soil type (5 classes) | 91.9–94.0 % | 44.6–46.8 % | **45.0–47.9 points** |
| Soil condition (4 classes) | 98.6–99.7 % | 75.5–86.1 % | 13.6–23.1 points |

Source-disjoint soil-type accuracy sits barely above the 43.3 % majority-class baseline.
Two mechanisms explain it: the same features identify *which photograph* a tile came from
with 91.5 % accuracy against a 6.3 % chance rate, and classes represented by a single
source photograph score an F1 of exactly zero once that photograph is held out.

The result held across three network backbones and five independent tiling seeds, with an
exact permutation test over photographs giving p = 3.1 × 10⁻⁵ for every backbone.

To our knowledge this gap had not previously been quantified for soil photographs.

## Relationship to the project report

The report summarises this finding in Sections 5.4 and 6.3. The paper is the full
treatment: bootstrap confidence intervals over source photographs, an exact sign-flip test,
a five-seed replication, per-class analysis, and a Monte Carlo evaluation of a proposed
probe-validity test.
